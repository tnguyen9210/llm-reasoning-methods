Four frontier strategies, four ways to spend the same budget.

Every method gets exactly 80 generations per question. What separates them is where in the tree those generations land — and the four mcts_bl_*_v02 variants diverge sharply. Measured across 20 single-question searches (4 methods × 5 level-5 problems spanning 5 MATH subjects).

cnt = PUCT · kube = KUBE density · kdepth = depth-discounted KUBE · sem = diversity-adjusted value

The four signatures — averaged over 5 questions

Where the 80 expansions land · depth profile per question

cnt kube kdepth sem — x: tree depth · y: # expansions at that depth

The shape is the strategy. kdepth piles a wall of expansions against the max-depth ceiling (depth 19–20) — it tunnels. sem keeps a broad, shallow hump centered at depth 3–6 — it fans out. cnt and kube sit between, with a mid-depth mode that tracks where high-value completions appear.

Exploration allocation · aggregate metrics

depth-Gini = concentration of expansions across depths (0 = spread evenly, 1 = piled at one depth). visit-Gini = concentration of visit counts across nodes (how much one subtree hoards attention). deep-half % = share of expansions below the midpoint depth. dead-ends = total nodes that hit the max-depth wall without completing (summed over 5 questions).

What the numbers say

Why each behaves this way

cntPUCT frontier

Selects on q + c·√(ln N_parent / N_leaf). The visit term pulls toward under-visited siblings, so exploration stays balanced and mid-depth — but on hard problems (Q5, Q49) it spreads thin and completes nothing: no leaf ever reaches EOS before the budget runs out.

kubeKUBE density

Selects on a value-density ratio (q+bonus)/cost. This favors cheap, high-value continuations, producing the highest completion count (avg 77) and the most balanced depth profile (lowest depth-Gini) — it neither tunnels nor stalls. The most robust all-rounder here.

kdepthdepth-discounted

Discounts the density by depth, which paradoxically rewards going deeper once a path looks alive. It tunnels: on Q0/Q5 it drives 150+ nodes into the max-depth wall (272 dead-ends total vs. ≤21 for any other method) and starves completions — yet on Q9 the same depth-seeking finds the answer fastest (156 comps).

semdiversity value

Selects on q + α·√(xᵀV⁻¹x) — a diversity bonus for semantically novel branches. This penalizes going deep down one line (deep nodes are semantically close to their parents), so it stays the shallowest (mean depth 6.98) and broadest. Trades completion volume for coverage.

Scope & caveats. One trial per (method, question), seed 100000, gen_budget=80, enforce_eager default, V100S. These are behavioral diagnostics on 5 problems — not accuracy claims; no scoring was run. "Completions" counts EOS/length-terminated leaves, not correct answers. The v02 terminal-fold mechanisms differ (cnt folds into backprop, sem into the diversity covariance V) but at this budget produced no dead-ends for either — the dead-end signal is entirely kdepth's depth-seeking and kube's occasional deep runs.
